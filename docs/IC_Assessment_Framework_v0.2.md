# IC Assessment Framework v0.2
*ClassTracker -- Australian Curriculum v9*

---

## Core Distinction: Guided Practice vs Independent Retrieval

A key insight underpinning this framework is the difference between **guided practice performance** and **independent retrieval**. Students can demonstrate strong understanding during supported, in-context activities (worksheets, guided tasks, worked examples) and then struggle when the scaffold is removed in a more independent assessment. This is a normal and well-documented part of the learning process, not a failure of teaching. The IC framework is designed to make this gap visible and actionable.

---

## Three-Phase Evidence Model

### Phase 1: Teaching Coverage
**Question: "Have we taught this?"**

The Instructional Component (IC) functions as a teaching checklist. When a teacher delivers an IC, it is logged as taught. No per-student outcome is required at this point. The goal is ensuring comprehensive curriculum coverage -- that all components of a Content Descriptor have been addressed.

- Recorded in: TaughtICs sheet
- Granularity: Class-level (IC taught yes/no)
- Teacher burden: Low -- single log entry per IC per session

---

### Phase 2: Check for Understanding
**Question: "Did they get it in context?"**

A lightweight, in-the-moment formative signal captured at the IC level. This could be an exit ticket, mini whiteboard check, thumbs up/down, or brief observation. The language is deliberately simple and non-judgemental.

**Outcome options:**
- **Got it** -- student demonstrated understanding in this context
- **Needs review** -- student needs another pass at this IC before moving on

This phase captures guided practice performance. It does not confirm mastery -- a student who "got it" in a supported context may still need further consolidation before independent demonstration.

- Recorded in: TaughtICs sheet (per-student IC outcome field)
- Granularity: Per student, per IC
- Teacher burden: Low -- quick class scan, not marking every piece of work

---

### Phase 3: Mastery Assessment
**Question: "Can they do it independently?"**

Triggered when a student has been taught 80% or more of the system default ICs for a descriptor. The app surfaces a prompt (Ready for Mastery banner) indicating the teacher now has sufficient teaching coverage to make a formal mastery judgement.

The teacher makes a **single holistic judgement** at the Content Descriptor level, drawing on their professional knowledge and accumulated evidence -- not a calculated average of IC scores. A marked exit ticket or short independent task is typically sufficient evidence at this point.

This phase captures independent retrieval performance, which is the meaningful evidence for descriptor-level mastery.

**Outcome options (descriptor level):**
- Mark as Mastered -- saved to Progress sheet
- Not yet ready:
  - Needs more practice (consolidation, no reteach required)
  - Needs reteaching (specific IC gaps identified)
  - Needs more evidence (teacher not yet confident in independent demonstration)

- Recorded in: Progress sheet
- Granularity: Per student, per Content Descriptor
- Teacher burden: Low -- holistic call, supported by Phase 1 and 2 data

---

## Why ICs Are a Teaching Instrument, Not an Assessment Instrument

IC coverage (Phase 1) is an administrative threshold, not an evidence threshold. A student at 80% IC coverage has been *exposed* to 80% of the teaching components for a descriptor. Exposure is necessary but not sufficient for mastery. The 80% gate exists to ensure the teacher has done enough teaching to make the Phase 3 judgement confidently -- it is a prompt, not a verdict.

The teacher remains the assessment instrument. The app surfaces evidence and prompts judgement; it does not automate it.

---

## Relationship to Achievement Standards

Content Descriptor mastery (Phase 3) aggregates upward to Achievement Standard judgements. A student who has demonstrated mastery across the descriptors linked to an Achievement Standard has the evidence base to support an AS-level judgement. This mirrors the AC v9 structure where descriptors elaborate the standard.
